# CaringBridge research report

Researched 2026-10-12 for the "Updates and Support" app decision. Labels used throughout:

- **[Verified]** — read directly on the cited page during this session.
- **[Reported]** — a third party says it; I did not confirm it independently.
- **[Inference]** — my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

**Method gap, stated up front:** Reddit was not reachable. The web search index returned no Reddit threads, and both the Reddit API and the in-app browser blocked reddit.com. The user voice below therefore comes from App Store reviews, an app-review aggregator, two patient forums, an Ask MetaFilter thread, a patient blog, and a Change.org petition. Treat the theme frequencies as indicative, not a census. Running a manual Reddit search before building is worth 20 minutes.

---

## TL;DR

1. **CaringBridge has no per-update audience control.** Privacy is set for the whole page (Public / Semi-Private / Private), and every visitor who can see the page sees every post. The default is **Semi-Private**, which means *any signed-in CaringBridge user* can read the page. That is a looser default than most families probably assume. [Verified]
2. **It has been shedding features.** A "new experience" rollout retired the **Planner** (December 2023, replaced by single-slot "Requests"), **Well Wishes**, the **Gallery**, and **text-message notifications**. There is **no private messaging**. App reviews from mid-2024 onward are full of update regret. [Verified]
3. **The economics are donations.** It is free, ad-free, and roughly 97% funded by contributions, about $10.7M in revenue in FY2024. The cost shows up as persistent donation prompts and "Tribute" donation wording that some visitors mistake for gifts to the family. [Verified]
4. **The ring concept already exists in at least one place:** Pfizer's free **LivingWith** app lets you put supporters into family / friends / wider-circle sub-circles and target each update or help request to one of them. It is cancer-only and US-only. [Verified] That makes the concept validated, not novel. Your differentiation has to come from execution, a crisis-first setup, and the supporter-to-supporter layer. [Inference]

## 1. What CaringBridge offers

**Framing** [Verified, caringbridge.org homepage]: the site pitches four pillars: Communicate, Capture, Coordinate, Community. It is "a no-cost, 501(c)(3) nonprofit health platform", founded in 1997, and claims more than 1 million pages created, more than 240,000 daily visitors, and about 1,200 support messages posted per hour.

### Posts (journal)
- Authors and co-authors write posts. Posts autosave as drafts and can be pinned, edited, and deleted. Photos and links are supported. [Verified, help center search results]
- A Requests tab sits alongside an All Activity feed. [Verified]

### Privacy controls — the key finding for you
[Verified, help article "differences between privacy settings"]
- There are **three levels, set for the whole page**:
  - **Public** — anyone can view; only signed-in users can comment.
  - **Semi-Private** — "All signed in users may see and respond to what you share." **This is the default for new sites**, and CaringBridge calls it the most common setting.
  - **Private** — only people on the Approved Visitors List can view. You can invite by email, approve access requests, turn access requests off, and remove (block) a visitor.
- **Separate toggles:** whether the page appears in CaringBridge's own search, in public search engines like Google, and whether visitors can share the page.
- **Password-protected pages are discontinued.**
- **No per-post audience.** I found no help article, feature description, or third-party review describing a way to show one post to a subset of visitors. The post-creation and privacy articles describe only page-level settings. One App Store reviewer claims you can share "with one specific person". I could not corroborate this and think it most likely describes the Private approved-visitor list. [Verified absence in docs; the reviewer claim is unconfirmed]
- **No private messaging:** "CaringBridge does not offer any private messaging at this time." [Verified]

### Supporter messages (formerly the "guestbook")
- Visitors **comment on posts** (text and photo), **react** with hearts, and can **reply to other comments**. Authors can see who reacted and who commented. Users can edit their own comments but not other people's. [Verified, help center]
- The old separate **Well Wishes** feature "has been removed from the new experience." [Verified]
- I found no documentation saying comments are hidden from other visitors. Because replies-to-comments exist, comments appear to be **visible to all page viewers**. That means CaringBridge already lets supporters see each other's messages, but only as threads under the author's posts, not as a standalone supporter space. [Inference, high confidence]

### Coordination
- **The Planner (a calendar of help tasks) was retired in December 2023.** [Verified, "Where did the Planner go?"]
- Its replacement, **Requests**, is a special post type with eight categories: Childcare, Chores, Errands, Meals, Pet Care, Transportation, Visiting, and Other. **"One person can pick up each request."** If you need several people or several dates, you create one request per slot. [Verified]
- There are integrations with **Meal Train**, **GoFundMe**, and **Instacart**, which link out to those services. [Verified, help center titles]
- CaringBridge claims that two-thirds of users get a matched offer of help within 24 hours of asking. [Verified that the claim exists; the methodology is CaringBridge's own survey]

### Notifications
- Email notifications for new posts, with a daily-summary option. **Text-message notifications are "not available at this time in the new experience."** [Verified]

### Mobile apps
- iOS: rated 4.9 from about 19,000 ratings. [Verified, App Store page, retrieved 2026-10-12]
- Android exists. A third-party summary puts its rating around 4.5. [Reported]

### Setup speed
- The signup flow (caringbridge.org/start) has you create a profile with email verification, then create the page. The signup flow is reported to require choosing a health condition from a drop-down. [Reported, Adam v. CaringBridge filings]
- An ACAP Community review from June 2026 lists "initial setup takes time, especially adding contacts for private updates" as a con. [Reported]
- My estimate is about 10 to 15 minutes to a first post on the default Semi-Private setting. Locking the page down to Private and inviting people one email at a time takes longer. [Inference]
- The help center's most-viewed article is **"How do I reset my password?" at about 220,000 views**, far ahead of every other article. That suggests sign-in friction is the dominant support burden. [Verified view count; the interpretation is inference]

## 2. Business and pricing model

- **Free, no ads, data "never sold"** per its homepage. [Verified]
- **Funding:** in FY2024, revenue was $10,712,173, of which $10,423,330 (about 97%) was contributions. Expenses were $11,608,997, a net loss of about $0.9M. Assets were $5.38M. [Reported, ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer summary of Form 990] FY2025 revenue was about $10.6M. [Reported, philanthropy.org]
- **Ratings:** 4-star Charity Navigator rating and BBB-accredited charity, per its own Financials page. [Verified that the claims are on the page]
- **Premium tiers:** none found. One aggregator review dated 2026-08-17 claims "Unless people pay $25, the service ends." [Reported, single unverified review] I found nothing corroborating a paywall, so it is likely a misread donation prompt. [Inference]
- **Instrumentation:** the public homepage loads FundraiseUp (a donation widget), Google Tag Manager, VWO (A/B testing), and the Facebook SDK. [Verified by inspecting the page source on 2026-10-12]

### Controversies
- **Donation prompts and "Tributes":** the donate prompts and "Tribute donation" wording lead some visitors to believe they are giving to the family. In fact, Tributes fund CaringBridge itself.
  - A Change.org petition (undated, older) says the author gave money believing it went to the family, and CaringBridge refunded it.
  - Aggregator reviewers call the wording "shady and deceptive". [Reported, justuseapp, undated]
  - CaringBridge now publishes a help article titled "Tribute donations vs. personal fundraising" with about 8,800 views, which suggests the confusion is common. [Verified view count]
  - Back in 2012, a MetaFilter user preferred CarePages partly because it lacked "the giant DONATE button". [Verified, 2012]
- **Tracking-pixel lawsuit:** *Adam v. CaringBridge* (N.D. Cal., No. 25-cv-06042) alleges that Meta Pixel and Google Analytics sent users' signup health-condition selections to third parties for advertising. On 2025-12-05 the court transferred the case to Minnesota under CaringBridge's forum-selection clause. **This was not a ruling on the merits, and I found no outcome after the transfer.** [Verified, dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com]
  - The App Store privacy label discloses name and email linked to the user for the developer's advertising or marketing. [Reported, search summary of the Apple listing]
  - Possibly stale: the case status may have moved since December 2025.

## 5. Gaps and opportunities for "Updates and Support"

### Per-update audience rings
- **CaringBridge does not have this.** Its finest control is the page-wide Private approved list. [Verified absence in documentation]
- **The concept is not unclaimed.** LivingWith ships a near-identical three-tier sub-circle model. [Verified]
- I found no evidence about LivingWith's adoption or user reception. It is pharma-branded and cancer-only, which likely limits it for "possible diagnosis, not yet cancer" cases and for non-cancer crises. [Inference]

### Supporters seeing each other's messages
- CaringBridge has this, but only as comment threads under posts. There is no standalone supporter wall, and the old Well Wishes feature was removed. [Verified/Inference as noted in §1]
- The opportunity is a supporter space that is **ring-aware**: close-friends messages are visible only to close friends. [Inference]

### Coordination
- CaringBridge **downgraded** here. The Planner calendar became single-taker Requests with no multi-slot scheduling, and the help center shows no sign of supporter-to-supporter coordination. [Verified/Inference]
- Your Phase 3 idea of supporters coordinating *with each other* fills a real hole, and it is also the space where Lotsa and Meal Train already compete. [Inference]

### Crisis-first setup
- CaringBridge's default is Semi-Private, visible to any CaringBridge user, and signup involves profile creation, email verification, and a health-condition selection. [Verified/Reported]
- A "first update out in under five minutes, private by default" flow is a credible wedge for the sudden-crisis moment. [Inference]

### Design risks to plan for (inferences drawn from the complaints)
1. **Ring leakage socially, not technically.** A friend in the "acquaintances" ring hears news secondhand and realizes they are outer-ring. Deciding whether ring membership is ever visible to supporters matters more than the access-control code.
2. **Ring-assignment burden at the worst moment.** Sorting 80 people into rings during a diagnosis week is real work. Consider defaulting everyone to one ring and letting refinement happen later.
3. **Reader-side friction kills it.** Login is CaringBridge's biggest support load. Magic-link or no-login reading for invited supporters would matter more than features.
4. **Trust and longevity.** CarePages' shutdown erased families' records. Data export, and a credible answer to "what happens if this app dies," should be in the first version.
5. **Health data plus tracking.** The CaringBridge pixel lawsuit is a warning. No ad pixels and no third-party analytics on health content is both cheaper and a marketing point.
6. **Emotional pressure.** Thompson's critique (the obligation to keep posting, no graceful way to stop) suggests features like "pause updates" or "last update for now" with a kind default message.

### Build-vs-use, framed for the decision
- **For a single family in crisis today:** CaringBridge on the **Private** setting, plus Meal Train or Lotsa for help, covers about 80% of the need with zero build time. Rings are the real gap. The workaround is two pages, or a separate group text for the inner circle. [Inference]
- **For building:** the unclaimed combination appears to be rings, a private-by-default fast setup, a ring-scoped supporter space, and multi-slot coordination, in a general (not cancer-only) app. [Inference]
